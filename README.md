# Storyhollow

*A hollow old tree where stories live, with a little glowing door.*

**A new story for every night.** Photograph the pages of a book your children love, or something wonderful
you found on a walk, and a cast of storytellers turns it into an immersive audio story, with a soft chime
whenever it's time to turn the page. Or start from an idea, and it becomes a picture book: words, voices and
pictures.

Built first for a shadow book whose old read-along had been heard a hundred times. Now every night can bring
a brand-new tale for the very same pictures.

**The family's Storyhollow:** [storyrecreation-production.up.railway.app](https://storyrecreation-production.up.railway.app)
(the live app; the test copy has its own address, in Railway's staging environment).

## What it does

### ✨ Create a New Story
- **From a book you own.** Snap each page (shadow books, picture books, board books). The storyteller studies
  the pictures and writes a *completely new* story that matches each page, so the child sees exactly what they
  hear. Old printed text in the photos is ignored, never reused.
- **From something you saw.** A puddle, a snail, a sofa-cushion castle: photos from your day become a story.
- **From just an idea.** "A dragon who's afraid of the dark."
- **Shape it:** length (3, 6 or 10 minutes), mood (cozy bedtime, silly, brave adventure, gentle mystery,
  heartwarming, spooky-ish), listener age, your child as the hero, a gentle lesson, special wishes, and a
  🎲 *Surprise us* twist.
- **Never the same twice.** Each book remembers its earlier stories, and the storyteller is told to make
  tonight's clearly different. Characters keep their voices from night to night.
- **Familiar friends, new adventures.** The characters keep the names printed in the book (say, Anton and
  Babette) unless you ask for new ones.
- **Pages in any order.** Photographed the book back to front? The storyteller reads the printed page numbers
  and puts the pages in order for this and every future story. There's also a ⇅ Reverse button.
- **✨ A new story tonight:** one tap on any book writes and records a fresh story with last time's settings.

### 🌍 Stories in French and Spanish
- **Choose the language** when shaping a story: English, Français or Español. The storyteller writes it all in
  that language (title, words and dialogue), the voices perform it in that language, and even the welcome and
  the ending are in it ("Bienvenue à Storyhollow…", "Fin.").
- **For someone learning it** (on to begin with): simple, everyday words and short sentences, key words repeated,
  at a gentle pace. Turn it off for a story told just as a French- or Spanish-speaking child would hear it.
- **The English is there when you want it.** Every page comes with its English too: under each page while you
  review the words, and in the player at a tap of **EN English**, under the words being read.
- **Books in French or Spanish are read in their own language.** *Read the book's own words* notices what
  language a photographed book is in, and reads it, welcome and ending included, in that language (with its
  English on screen); words you paste or photograph are read in their language too.
- **A voice that sounds at home.** Every voice can speak French and Spanish; for the most natural accent,
  *Conjure a voice* can make one speaking the language, and it's cast for stories in it.
- "Make one like…" and *A new story tonight* keep the language, and story cards say *en français* or
  *en español*.

### 📖 Picture books
- **An idea becomes an illustrated book.** For a story dreamed up from an idea, switch on *Make it a picture book*
  and pick a style: watercolor, painted, colored pencil, cut paper or shadow puppets.
- **Five styles that really look different.** The style is the first thing the illustrator is told, in detail (cut
  paper is shapes cut from flat colored papers, with crisp scissor edges and little shadows, and no outlines or
  painted shading), and every sketch, cover and page is drawn in it. A character brought back from another book
  keeps the style it was drawn in, and says so; for pages all in the new style, sketch them fresh.
- **The pictures come first.** Once a picture book's story is recorded, its pictures lead the page: meeting the
  characters and drawing the book is what's next. *Listen now, before the pictures* is just below for anyone who
  can't wait; until a page's picture is drawn it shows the cover, and each picture joins the story as it's made,
  with no need to record it again. The pictures stay where they are while they're drawn.
- **Meet the characters first.** The storyteller reads the finished story and plans the pictures: who's in them,
  what each one looks like, and what every page shows. **Sketch everyone at once**: one approval for two sketches
  of each main character (untick anyone you don't want drawn), then choose a favorite for each. Or photograph your
  child's own drawing and the sketches are made from it.
- **Characters you've drawn before come back, free.** A character with the same name as one chosen for another
  of your picture books (your children, say) is offered again on the new book, at no cost.
- **Change any page before it's drawn.** Beside each page's words is what its picture will show; change it in
  your own words ("only two beds"), free, before drawing the book.
- **The same on every page.** The cover is drawn first, then every page from the chosen sketches and the cover,
  so the characters and colors stay the same all the way through. Redraw any page, with a note about what to change.
- **You can see it working.** Whatever the illustrator is making shows where it will appear, with how many are
  ready and about how long is left, and a note follows you down the page until it's done (it's fine to leave, too).
  *The book* shows the cover and every page at a glance. Tap any sketch or picture to see it full size, and swipe
  between them.
- **It finishes what you approved.** A picture that doesn't come for a passing reason (Google busy, an empty reply)
  is tried again by itself, on the same purchase. If the app restarts midway (an update, say), it carries on where
  it stopped. If a picture still can't be drawn, it says why right beside it, and whether that cost anything.
- **Children are described, never photographed:** the illustrator works from words like "curly hair and a
  brand-new gap in a big smile". A photo can help write those words (*Describe from a photo*): the storyteller
  looks at it once and writes the description, which you can change; the photo isn't kept, and the illustrator
  only ever sees the words.
- **Living pictures:** while a story plays, its picture drifts slowly, and a little touch the storyteller chose
  for the scene (fairy dust, falling snow, fireflies, petals…) floats over it. Photographed books get it too. It
  pauses with the story, and rests on phones set to reduce motion.
- **The price first.** Every step that costs money says what it costs before it happens, priced from what your
  recent pictures really cost (Google's charges vary a little), so an approved purchase covers every picture in
  it. On a device where the PIN was entered lately, a tap says yes; otherwise the PIN is asked for. The
  illustrator never spends more than was approved, and the Grown-ups' corner shows what was spent.
- **Try it before setting it up:** until the illustrator's key is added, free stand-in pictures are drawn instead.

### 🎭 Voices that feel human
- **A full cast.** The narrator and every character get their own expressive voice from ElevenLabs'
  newest model (Eleven v4), performed as one scene, so turn-taking sounds like real actors and not a robot
  reading lines.
- **Directed delivery.** The script includes cues like `[whispers]`, `[giggles]` and `[sleepily]`, which the
  voices act out.
- **Storybook personas.** Kids pick *Professor Hoot*, *Granny Willow*, *Captain Bramble*… not "voice #12".
- **🪄 Conjure a voice.** Describe one ("a tiny, squeaky mouse who talks very fast"), hear three versions, and
  keep your favorite in the family's voice wardrobe for future stories. A voice can be made speaking English,
  French or Spanish, with a natural accent.
- **Make one right where you choose.** Every storyteller list ends with **🪄 Make a new voice** (“Someone you
  love, from a voicemail or a video, or one you dream up”), which asks which: **💛 Someone you love** (a family
  voice, below) or **🪄 Dream one up**. Either is made right there, without leaving the page or losing anything
  chosen on it, and is picked as the storyteller. A dreamed-up voice is made in the story's language. Lists of
  character voices go straight to dreaming one up, since a family voice only ever tells the story. The filters
  (favorites, family, made by you…) wrap onto a second line on a phone, so every one is in view. On a story's
  page, each character shows who voices them, with **✎ Change**.

### 💛 Family voices
- **Keepsakes, kept just as they are.** Upload a voicemail, a voice memo or a video, or record right in the app:
  Grandma's birthday message, your children reading at four. They're tidied into good MP3s in the browser (192 kbps,
  the quality ElevenLabs asks for to learn a voice) and stay private to your family.
- **Videos give up their sound, and nothing else.** Pick a video from your photos (an iPhone's .MOV, or any MP4):
  its sound is taken out right there on the phone, and only that is sent and kept. The video itself never leaves
  the phone. Long ones are welcome: up to 20 minutes each, however big the video, as many as you like. From a
  longer one (a video or a voice memo), you listen and slide a three-minute window to where they're speaking (a
  minute to three is all a voice needs), or make it longer, up to 20 minutes, to keep more.
- **Or straight from a Google Drive link.** Paste the link to a video in Google Drive (shared with “Anyone with
  the link”): its sound comes straight from Google to the phone, never through Storyhollow, and only that is
  fetched and kept, so a big video is no trouble.
  It can be made private again afterwards. If the link isn't shared that way, or is a folder, it says what to do.
  (This needs a Google key, set up once: see *Videos from Google Drive* below.)
- **🎙️ Record now, with a script made for it.** For a storytelling voice, *Record now* shows a two-minute bedtime
  story, *The Lantern in the Hollow*, written to hold every sound of English, with questions, a surprise, a chuckle
  and a whisper, and tips for recording it well: a quiet room, the phone a hand's width away, your own reading
  voice, and no need to start over after a stumble. The recording is kept as it sounds in the room (none of the
  processing meant for calls), the screen stays on while you read, and the timer says when it's enough.
- **✂️ Keep just part of it.** Before keeping a recording, slide *From* and *To* to cut away the start or the end
  (*Hear just that part* to check). There's no need to cut one for a storytelling voice: that's chosen when the
  voice is made.
- **A folder for each person.** In the Grown-ups' corner, everyone's recordings are kept together under their
  name ("4 recordings · 2:13 · reads stories as “Gram T”"). Open a folder to play a recording (one at a time,
  with a slider to move through it), add another of theirs, remove one, or let them read stories.
- **Found where it matters, without getting in the way.** Until there's a family voice, every storyteller list
  (making a story, *Read to Me*, a story's *Who should read it?*) ends with a small *Did you know?*: someone you
  love can read to you, from an old voicemail or a video, with *Make their voice →* (✕ puts it away on that
  phone). *Read to Me*'s own-words step and the welcome page mention it too. Once there is one, they come first in
  every storyteller list, and their folder offers *Pick something to read* or *A new story, read by them*,
  which open with them already chosen.
- **Hear the voice that was made, not the recording.** *▶ Hear me* on a family voice, and *▶ Hear how they read*
  in their folder, play that voice reading a few lines of a bedtime story (about fifteen seconds, a whisper and a
  smile in it). It's made once, as soon as the voice is (or the first time it's asked for: "⏳ A moment…"), for a
  few seconds of voice credit, and kept with the family's files (`src/lib/server/voiceSample.ts`).
- **Made right where the storyteller is chosen.** *Someone you love* (under **🪄 Make a new voice**) and *Make
  their voice →* open a window over the page, in two steps: a recording of them (a file, a video, a Google Drive
  link, or *Record now*), then their voice learned. Someone with recordings already goes straight to the second
  step, and someone who already reads stories can simply be chosen. The voice that's made becomes the storyteller
  (“💛 Grandma June can read stories now.”), and whatever else was chosen on the page stays as it was.
- **A grown-up's voice can read anything.** ElevenLabs instant voice cloning learns their voice from their
  recordings, and they then appear first in every storyteller list, ready to read a book, a poem or a letter.
  Making one asks the grown-up to confirm the voice is an adult's and that the family has the right to use it
  (they agreed, or, for someone who has passed away, the family is at peace with it).
- **Their clearest few minutes, not every minute.** Instant cloning learns best from a minute to three minutes of
  someone speaking clearly on their own; past about three minutes, ElevenLabs says, more doesn't help and can make
  the voice less steady. So the voice maker adds up what's chosen and says when it's just right, or more than it
  needs. A long recording can lend just its best part: tap **✂️ Just part** beside it and slide to the stretch
  where they're talking on their own. Only that part is sent to learn from; the recording itself stays whole.
- **Never cast by accident.** A family voice only reads when you pick it as the storyteller; the casting
  director never hands Grandma the troll's lines. Children's recordings stay keepsakes and are never cloned.

### 🔔 Made for real books
- **A gentle page-turn chime** (bell, wand sparkle, or wind chime) with an adjustable pause after it.
- **The classic read-along welcome:** *"You'll know it's time to turn the page when you hear this sound…"*
- **✋ Wait for me:** after each chime the story pauses until someone taps, for slow little page-turners.
- **🌙 Lights out:** a near-black screen with ember-dim controls and words, so the phone doesn't wash out a
  shadow book on the bedroom wall. It's off until you turn it on.
- **Aa Read-along words:** each word lights up as it's spoken, right under the picture of the page, which
  helps early readers.
- **🖼️ Big picture:** turn a phone sideways, or tap the picture, and it fills the screen (the words step aside;
  a tap brings back the controls). Tap the smaller-picture button to have the words again.
- **"The end."** Every story ends with the storyteller saying so, so everyone knows it's over (a classic read
  chapter by chapter says it's the end of the chapter, until the last).
- **🦉 Sound effects on cue** (optional): a sound designer picks a few moments, like a door's creak on
  "creaked" or an owl's hoot, and each short sound lands on its word, softly beneath the voices. Turn
  sounds on or off for any finished story without re-recording the voices.
- **One MP3** with narration, chimes and pauses baked in, so it plays on a lock screen, a Bluetooth speaker or
  in the car, and can be **downloaded for a make-your-own Yoto card**.

### 🎧 Read to Me
- **Pick a book**, then who reads it (whoever read that book last is picked again, so chapter 3 sounds like
  chapters 1 and 2). Add *character voices* so the dialogue is acted out.
- **The Classic Treasury:** Peter Rabbit, Winnie-the-Pooh, The Velveteen Rabbit, The Wind in the Willows,
  Aesop, Grimm and more, plus a search of 70,000+ public-domain books (Project Gutenberg).
- **A book we own:** choose a book you've added (photographed once, under *Add a book*) and the storyteller
  transcribes the real words exactly, then reads them aloud with a chime per page.
- **Our own words:** write, paste, or add a photo or screenshot of a poem, a letter from Granny, a birthday card,
  or a story your child dictated. The storyteller copies the words out of the picture for you to check first.

### 📚 The Library
- A candlelit bookcase. Books stand face-out on shelves (Shadow Books, Picture Books, Tales from the Wild,
  Dreamed-Up Tales, The Classic Treasury, or any shelf you name), and each book holds all its stories.
- **One bookcase, books all the way down.** What a child would call a book is a book: a dreamed-up tale is one
  book, and a book you own holds every story made for it (open it to see them). Books stand face-out, three to a
  shelf on a phone, each kind under its own brass plaque (small kinds share a shelf on a computer), or all
  together newest first or A to Z with **Arrange**. **Search** finds a book by its title or any of its stories',
  and **⭐ Favorites** shows the books you've starred (tap the ☆ on a book's cover, on its page; children can too)
  and any book holding a story starred while it played. A favorite wears a little gold star on the shelves, and
  Home's *Favorites* has its newest story ready to play. The bookcase is the whole family's, so
  there's no choosing whose: Home is where each child finds their own stories.
- **Folders for books that belong together** (a series, the same two characters, a season's books): tap
  **📁 Make a folder**, name it, tick its books, and they stand on the shelf as one wooden box with the tops of
  their covers peeking out. Tap it to open it; *Change this folder* renames it, adds or takes out books, or
  unpacks it (the books go back on their shelves; nothing is deleted). A search still finds every book, in a
  folder or not, and a new story made *like* one in a folder goes in the same folder.
- **Every cover says its name.** A dreamed-up tale's cover is drawn without words (and one from the wild is your
  photo), so its name is written across the top. A dreamed-up book you didn't name takes its first story's
  title once the storyteller has written it, instead of the first words of the idea.
- To rename a book, move it to another shelf or folder, or take it off the shelves, open it and tap *Rename, move
  or remove*.
- **Family accounts with reader cards:** one grown-up login per family, then a "Who's listening tonight?"
  card for each child (no passwords), so Home greets them by name with their own stories.
- **Grown-up PIN** (optional): children can play anything on the shelves, but creating new stories, buying
  pictures and changing settings need a grown-up (buying pictures needs a PIN, so a family chooses one the first
  time). Like a phone's passcode, it's checked the moment the last digit goes in. Once it's entered, the device
  doesn't ask again for half an hour, or for 30 days with *Don't ask again on this device* (ticked to begin with).
  **🔒 Lock now** in the Grown-ups' corner makes it ask every time again.
- **⬇ Download everything:** one ZIP with every story as an MP3 (filed by book), the family recordings (by
  person), the book photos, and a *Read me* page to browse, play and read every story's words. It's the
  family's copy to keep, whatever happens to the app or the subscriptions.
- **📄 A PDF book to share:** right under a finished story on its book's page, *📄 Share as a PDF* (beside *⬇️ Save
  the MP3*), or *Make a PDF book to share* on the story's own page, makes it a book of its own: a
  cover, each page's picture big across the top with its words just beneath (what the characters say in quotation
  marks, as a printed book has it), and *The End*. A picture book's pages are made to fit its pictures, ten inches
  across (the pictures are drawn wider than they're tall) and only as tall as the words need, so there's little
  empty paper; a story without pictures is six by nine inches. On a phone that can share files, *📤 Share it* sends it by
  Messages, email or AirDrop; anywhere else it's saved with the downloads. A picture book has its own pictures, and
  a story of something you saw has your photos. A new story for a book you own has its words alone, page by page,
  to read along with your copy: that book's pictures belong to its publisher, so they stay in the library. A
  reading of a book you own has no PDF, since its words are the book's own. A story in French or Spanish has each
  page's English beneath it, and a classic's chapter with more to come ends *To be continued…*.
- **Every wait says so.** A tap that opens a page shows at once that it's on its way; writing and recording a
  story say about how long is left (and if it's taking longer than usual). The time shown while a story is
  written counts the voices too, so it's the wait until it's ready to hear, and every wait learns from how long the
  last dozen like it really took, so it gets truer as you go. When many families are making stories at once, a
  story waiting its turn for the voices says its place in line and about how long. The play button's ring turns while
  a story loads on a weak signal. Adding sound effects to a recorded story counts only the new sounds (the
  voices are kept). Conjuring a voice, teaching the storytellers a family voice, and re-binding a story with
  new sounds each say about how long they take. Every upload says how far along it is (a child's drawing,
  words from a photo, a family recording) and is sent again by itself if the signal drops.
  **Download everything** says it's getting the backup ready, then that the download has started and how big
  it is, and asks for the PIN first if it has run out.
- **Adding a book on a weak signal.** A new book's photos go up one at a time ("Sending page 3 of 8…", with a
  bar), and the grown-up PIN is asked before they go. A photo the signal drops, or a connection that stops
  moving for 40 seconds, is simply sent again; if the signal is gone, it says how many photos are in, and
  **Try again** carries on from there. The book is made once they're all in, so it's never shelved twice.

## Quick start

You need **Node.js 22.13+**.

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env.local     # add your two API keys
npm run dev                    # open http://localhost:3000
```

| Key | What it powers | Where to get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` | The storyteller (Claude reads the photos and writes the scripts) | [console.anthropic.com](https://console.anthropic.com) |
| `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` | The voices, voice design and sound effects | [elevenlabs.io → API keys](https://elevenlabs.io/app/settings/api-keys) |

**No keys yet?** Run `DEMO_MODE=1 npm run dev` to click through every screen with a pretend storyteller and
humming placeholder voices.

### Your first shadow book story
1. Sign up and add a reader card for each child.
2. **Library → ＋ Add a book.** Name it, choose *Shadow book*, and photograph each picture **in order**: the
   slide or disc itself, or its projection on the wall. One photo per page. The ← → buttons reorder pages.
   (Adding a book is always done here, once; Create and Read to Me send you here and bring you back.)
3. On the book's page, tap **🎨 Choose the details** (or **🎧 Read the book's own words** to hear it as printed).
4. Choose the length, mood and voices. Leave *Let me read it before the voices record it* on the first time.
5. Read the script, swap any voice, then press **Record the story**. After a minute or two, press **Listen**.
6. Tomorrow, open the book and tap **✨ Quick create**. When a story ends, *Up next* offers the next one.
7. For a picture book, choose **Just an idea**, describe it (the sillier the what-ifs, the better), and switch on
   **📖 Make it a picture book**. Once it's written, meet the characters on the story's page: **Sketch everyone
   at once**, tap the sketches to see them big and choose together, look over what each page will show, then
   **Draw the book**. Each of those shows its price and how long it takes before you say yes.
8. To make one like a story you've made before, open Create: *Quick create* at the bottom offers the last story
   made for the book you choose, or the last of the kind you choose (*Choose another* lists, and searches, every
   story you've made). Its storyteller, length, mood, sounds and characters are filled in, so it's one tap; *Start
   fresh* clears them. It's for the start: once you begin describing your own idea or adding photos, it steps
   aside. **Make another like this** on a story's page does the same, with its book already chosen.
9. Create and Read to Me show your 6 most recent books, with a search and *See all* for the rest; once you pick
   one it folds to a single line, with *Change*.

## How it works

```
 page photos ──► Claude (vision + structured output) ──► script: cast, voices, lines per page
                                                             │
                     grown-up review & edits (optional) ◄────┘
                                                             │
 ElevenLabs Text-to-Dialogue (Eleven v4, PCM + timestamps) ◄─┘   one request per page
                                                             │
 mixer: welcome · page 1 · chime · pause · page 2 · … ◄──────┘   + optional sound effects, each on its word
                                                             │
 story.mp3 + page timeline + word timings ──► player (page cues, wait-for-me, read-along, lock screen)
```

- **Storyteller:** `claude-opus-5-5` with adaptive thinking, a JSON-schema output whose `voice_id` field is
  restricted to the family's real voices, and automatic refusal fallbacks. Prompts:
  `src/lib/server/storyteller.ts`.
- **Voices:** ElevenLabs Text-to-Dialogue performs each page as one multi-voice scene, returning raw PCM
  plus per-character timings (the source of the read-along). If dialogue isn't available on a plan, it
  falls back to line-by-line speech. See `src/lib/server/performer.ts`.
- **Chimes** are synthesized in code (`src/lib/shared/chime.ts`), so the preview in the browser is exactly
  what gets mixed into the story.
- **Mixing and MP3 encoding** are pure JavaScript (`src/lib/server/audio.ts`); no ffmpeg needed.
- **A video's sound** is taken out in the browser (`src/lib/client/videoSound.ts`). Safari opens an MP4 or M4A for
  its sound but not a QuickTime movie, which is what an iPhone records. So the movie's tables are read for where
  its AAC sound is kept, just that is read from the file (a tenth of a megabyte of a 250 MB video), and it's packed,
  untouched, as a plain .m4a for the browser to open. A voice memo or voicemail saved as .m4a is read the same way.
  From one longer than 20 minutes, only the part the family chooses is opened. The sound is opened two minutes at a
  time into one mono track (each piece from six samples before it, and joined where it matches the end of the last
  one, sample for sample), and the MP3 is made a block at a time, so twenty minutes needs a quarter of the room it
  would opened whole. From a Drive link the same pieces are fetched with ranged requests, never more than about
  2 MB at a time, falling back to the whole file (up to 400 MB) if Google won't send pieces. Any other kind of file
  is opened whole, as before.
- **Editing a page only re-records that page.** Recordings are cached by content, and changing the chime
  or the sounds re-binds the story without re-recording any voices.
- **Pictures:** a separate Claude request reads a picture book's finished script and plans its pictures (each
  character's look, each page's scene). Google's picture model (`gemini-nano-banana-2.1` through the Gemini API)
  draws the sketches, then the cover, then each page with the chosen sketches and the cover as reference pictures.
  Every picture is drawn on a purchase a grown-up approved; a job sets each picture's price aside before drawing it
  and stops before it would pass what was approved. See `src/lib/server/illustrator.ts` and `src/lib/server/art.ts`.
- **Kept for children.** What a family types for the storyteller (a story's idea and wishes, words for *Read to Me*
  to read aloud, words changed in a story before it's recorded, a voice described) is read first by a small, quick
  model (`GUARD_MODEL`, Claude Haiku by default; about a tenth of a cent a check), and anything no children's book
  would hold (sex, drugs or alcohol, self-harm, graphic violence, cruelty played for fun, hate, swearing, or attempts
  to talk the storyteller out of its rules) is turned down before anything is written or spent, while the hard things
  children meet (a pet dying, a hospital stay, a bully at school) stay welcome, handled gently. The storyteller keeps
  the same rules, and says (`for_children`, first in its answer, so no page is voiced) when the photos or a book's
  pages themselves aren't for children. Without the storyteller, a plain screen of unmistakable words stands in. The
  Grown-ups' corner lists everything turned down, with who asked (*Child safety*). See
  `src/lib/server/guard.ts`.
- **What things cost** is worked out from each service's reported usage (`src/lib/server/prices.ts`) and kept in
  the database (`src/lib/server/spending.ts`), so estimates follow what the family's stories have really cost.
- **Conjuring quickly.** ElevenLabs makes four recordings at once (`VOICES_AT_ONCE`, shared by every story being
  made: one under the Creator plan's limit of 5, so a voice being designed still gets a turn; raise it on a bigger
  plan, to 9 on Pro). The welcome, the soundscape and the sound effects are made alongside the pages, each page's
  sound effects as soon as its words are timed. When nobody's reading a story over first, each page is voiced the
  moment the storyteller has written it, while it writes the rest (`watchPages` in `src/lib/server/storyteller.ts`):
  never the last page, which gets "The end", and never twice, since a recording asked for while it's being made is
  waited for (`cachedPerformance` in `src/lib/server/jobs.ts`).
- **Many families at once.** Every story shares the voices through one fair line (`src/lib/server/line.ts`): the
  story started first has its turns first, and a story waiting with nothing being made for it says where it stands
  ("Waiting for a free storyteller · 3rd in line") and about how long, the time it waits added to the wait it was
  told (and left out of the times the waits learn from). A family makes one story at a time, so no family can fill
  the line. When the storyteller is busy (Anthropic's limit for the minute), a story waits and asks again for up to
  about three minutes, saying so, instead of failing. Finished stories are put together one at a time, and the
  mixing and MP3 give way to everything else every few milliseconds, so the app never stops answering while a story
  is bound. A story that an update or restart stops midway carries on by itself once the app is back, keeping the
  pages already voiced (one stopped twice is let go, with a word to try again).
- **How long things take** is learned the same way: each finished writing, recording, planning, sketching and
  drawing job is kept beside its rule of thumb (`src/lib/server/timing.ts`), and later waits go at the pace the last
  dozen really went, with a fifth to spare. Until three have been timed, the rule of thumb stands. The test copy and
  the live app each learn on their own.
- **PDF books** are made on the server with pdf-lib (`src/lib/server/pdf.ts`), in the app's own type (IM Fell
  English for titles, Andika for the words, from the font packages the app already loads). Each picture is turned
  the right way up and made just big enough for the page, as a JPEG (with sharp), so a picture book stays a few
  megabytes, small enough to send. A story with pictures gets pages fitted to them: 10 inches across for wide
  pictures (7½ for tall ones), each picture the full width, and the page as tall as the picture plus the words of
  all but the wordiest quarter of its pages, in 14-point type. A wordier page makes do with 13 or 12 point, then a
  smaller picture, and only if that won't do does the picture get a page to itself with the words after. Words alone
  (13½ point, six by nine inches) flow from page to page, and a paragraph never leaves one line alone at the top or
  foot of a page.
- **Sound effects** are placed by a small, separate Claude request that reads the finished script
  (`spotSoundEffects` in `src/lib/server/storyteller.ts`), made with ElevenLabs Sound Effects, and laid on
  the spoken word's timing about 8 dB under the narration (`src/lib/server/audio.ts`).
- **Background jobs** keep writing and recording if a phone locks or the page is closed. If the server
  restarts mid-job, the story shows *Try again* and resumes where it stopped.

## Your questions, answered

**Is this better as an app?**
Start as an installable web app (what this is). On iPhone or iPad tap *Share → Add to Home Screen*; on
Android, *Install app*. It opens full-screen with its own icon, the camera works for photographing pages,
audio keeps playing on the lock screen, and every update arrives instantly with no app store review. If you
later want App Store distribution, wrap the same code with Capacitor; nothing needs rewriting.

**Does it work without signal?**
The stories you've listened to, yes. Each story is saved on the phone the first time it plays (its recording,
page and pictures; the 25 most recent are kept), so in the car or on a plane it opens and plays as usual.
A page that wasn't saved shows *No signal just now* with the saved stories to choose from. Making new
stories needs a connection. Signing out removes everything saved on the phone.

**Accounts?**
Built in as a *family account* (one grown-up email and password) with *reader cards* for each child, like a
streaming service's profile picker. Books and stories belong to the family, on one bookcase everyone shares;
each story remembers who it was made for, and Home shows each child their own.

**Can it find books in a library database?**
Partly. Out-of-copyright classics come straight from Project Gutenberg's free catalogue (the Classic
Treasury). Modern picture books are still under copyright and aren't available as text from any open
database or library API, so for those you photograph your own copy and the app reads its real words aloud.
That's for your family's own listening; please don't share those recordings publicly.

## Hosting it for the family

### On Railway (recommended, about $5 a month)

1. **Get your keys:** an Anthropic API key (console.anthropic.com → Billing → buy credits, $5 minimum →
   API Keys → Create Key) and an ElevenLabs API key (elevenlabs.io → pick a plan → API Keys).
2. Sign in at [railway.com](https://railway.com) with GitHub and choose the **Hobby** plan ($5/month, which
   includes $5 of usage; Storyhollow normally fits inside it).
3. **New Project → Deploy from GitHub repo →** allow access to this repository and pick it.
4. If the repository's main branch isn't the one with Storyhollow, open the service's **Settings → Source**
   and choose the right branch.
5. **Add a volume** to the service with mount path **`/data`**. That's where books, photos and recordings live.
6. **Variables:** add `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`, `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` and `SIGNUP_CODE` (a secret word only your
   family knows; it's needed to create a library card). For picture books, add `GEMINI_API_KEY` too (see
   *Picture books* below); without it, stand-in pictures are drawn for free.
7. **Settings → Networking → Generate Domain.** That's your Storyhollow address (`…up.railway.app`).
8. Open it, create your library card with the sign-up code, and on the iPad use **Share → Add to Home Screen**.

Railway rebuilds automatically whenever the branch is updated. Leave "serverless / app sleeping" off: stories
are written and recorded in the background.

### Anywhere else

The camera and "Add to Home Screen" need **HTTPS** (or `localhost`).

- **Docker** (a home server, NAS, Fly.io, Render…). Mount a volume for `/data`:
  ```bash
  docker build -t storyhollow .
  docker run -p 3000:3000 -v storyhollow-data:/data \
    -e ANTHROPIC_API_KEY=… -e ELEVENLABS_API_KEY=… -e SIGNUP_CODE=… storyhollow
  ```
- On a home network over plain `http://`, add `-e INSECURE_COOKIES=1` so sign-in cookies work without HTTPS.
- It needs a persistent disk (SQLite + audio files) and a long-running Node process for background jobs, so
  serverless-only hosts aren't a fit as-is.

**Who can sign up:** with `SIGNUP_CODE` set, creating a library needs the code. Without it, only the first
family can sign up and the door closes behind them, so strangers who find the address can't spend your credits.

Optional settings live in `.env.example` (`STORY_MODEL`, `VOICE_MODEL`, `VOICES_AT_ONCE`, `ELEVENLABS_BASE_URL`,
`DATA_DIR`). On a bigger ElevenLabs plan, set `VOICES_AT_ONCE` one under its limit (9 on Pro) to make stories faster
still.

### Picture books

The illustrator is Google's picture model, through the Gemini API:

1. Go to [aistudio.google.com](https://aistudio.google.com), sign in with a Google account, and choose **Get API key →
   Create API key**.
2. Pictures are paid, so turn on billing for the key's project (AI Studio offers to set it up next to the key).
   Google bills the pictures to that account.
3. In Railway, open the service's **Variables** and add `GEMINI_API_KEY` with the key. (Paste it there, never into a
   chat or an email.) Add it to the test copy first and try a picture book there.

| Variable | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `GEMINI_API_KEY` | The key from Google AI Studio (`GOOGLE_API_KEY` works too) |
| `PICTURE_MODEL` | Optional; defaults to `gemini-nano-banana-2.1`. `gemini-3-pro-image-preview` (Nano Banana Pro) paints richer pictures at about three times the price |
| `PICTURE_PRICE` | Optional; dollars per picture, for a picture model the app doesn't know the price of |

The Grown-ups' corner shows whether the illustrator is connected, about what a picture costs, and what was spent.

### Videos from Google Drive

To add a family video by pasting its Google Drive link, the phone fetches it straight from Google with a key made
for browsers:

1. Open [console.cloud.google.com](https://console.cloud.google.com) with your Google account and pick a project
   (the one AI Studio made for the Gemini key is fine), or create one.
2. **APIs & Services → Library**, search for **Google Drive API**, and **Enable** it.
3. **APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → API key**, then edit the key:
   - **Application restrictions → Websites**: add your addresses, e.g.
     `https://storyrecreation-production.up.railway.app/*` and the test copy's address.
   - **API restrictions → Restrict key → Google Drive API** only.
4. In Railway, add `GOOGLE_DRIVE_API_KEY` with the key (to the test copy first).

This is a different key from the Gemini one. Browsers see it, which is why it's limited to your addresses and can
only read files already shared with “Anyone with the link”. Never use the Gemini key here. Without it, the
*Or paste a Google Drive link* option simply doesn't appear.

### Automatic backups

Every night, the app can copy everything (the database, every story's audio, family recordings and book photos)
to S3-compatible cloud storage you own, away from the server: Backblaze B2 (10 GB free), Cloudflare R2 or Amazon
S3. Make a **private** bucket and a key that can read and write only that bucket, then set:

| Variable | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `BACKUP_ENDPOINT` | The storage's S3 address, e.g. `s3.us-west-004.backblazeb2.com` |
| `BACKUP_BUCKET` | The bucket's name |
| `BACKUP_KEY_ID` | The key's ID (Backblaze: keyID) |
| `BACKUP_SECRET_KEY` | The key's secret (Backblaze: applicationKey) |
| `BACKUP_REGION` | Optional; read from Backblaze and Amazon addresses, `auto` otherwise |

Backups start a few minutes after the app does, then run whenever the last good one is a day old (a failed one is
tried again within the hour). After the first, only new or changed files are sent. The Grown-ups' corner shows how
the last one went and has a **Back up now** button. If `BACKUP_BUCKET` doesn't match a bucket, the message names
the buckets the key can see (the storage is asked), so the right name can be copied in without signing in to the
storage's website. Each copy of the app keeps its own folder in the bucket
(`production/`, `staging/`), holding `files/` (the data folder) and `database/` (the latest copy plus one a day for
two weeks; the Gutenberg catalogue is left out, since it's fetched again by itself).

**Bringing a backup back:** with the app stopped and the same four variables set,

```bash
node scripts/restore-backup.mjs /data                      # this copy's latest backup
node scripts/restore-backup.mjs --date 2026-10-21 /data    # the database as it was that day
node scripts/restore-backup.mjs --from production /data    # e.g. fill the test copy with the live library
```

It won't write over an existing library unless you add `--force`.

### Problem alerts

The app can email you when something goes wrong that you'd otherwise find at bedtime: a story that didn't
finish writing or recording (with ElevenLabs' or Claude's reason, and a link back to it), a nightly backup that
failed, voice credit under a tenth of the month's, or the storyteller's Anthropic credit used up (once a day while
it lasts). It sends through [Resend](https://resend.com): make a free
account with the address the alerts should go to, create a key with sending access, and set:

| Variable | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `RESEND_API_KEY` | The Resend key |
| `ALERT_EMAIL` | Where alerts go (the address the Resend account was made with) |
| `ALERT_FROM` | Optional; defaults to Resend's `onboarding@resend.dev`, which can only send to that address |
| `PUBLIC_URL` | Optional; the app's address for links in emails (on Railway it's found by itself) |

The same problem is sent once (a failed story at most once an hour, a failing backup once a day, low credit once
a month), and never more than 20 emails an hour. The test copy's emails start with "🧪 Test copy". The Grown-ups'
corner shows whether alerts are on, the last few sent, and a **Send a test email** button.

**If the app itself is down** it can't send anything, so point a free uptime monitor (UptimeRobot, Better Stack)
at `https://<your app>/api/health`, every 5 minutes. It answers `{"ok":true}` when the app is up and its library
can be read, and `503` when not.
